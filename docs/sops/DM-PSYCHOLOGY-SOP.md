# The MagnetEngine DM Psychology Playbook

*How to turn cold Instagram DMs into booked calls — the psychology behind every
message you approve in your queue.*

> **How to use this doc:** read it once before your first campaign, then keep
> it open while you review DMs in the Approval Queue. Every principle here maps
> to something you do inside MagnetEngine (approving DMs, editing openers,
> setting follow-up sequences, updating your CRM tracker).

---

## 1. The one rule that governs everything

**The goal of a cold DM is a conversation, not a sale.**

Nobody buys from a first message. The only job of DM #1 is to earn a reply.
The only job of the replies that follow is to earn a call. Every psychological
principle below serves that chain:

```
DM → reply → short conversation → booked call → close (on the call, not in the DM)
```

If a message you're about to approve tries to skip a step (pitching the offer,
dropping a price, pasting a link in message #1), reject it or edit it back.

## 2. Why people reply — 6 principles that do the work

### 2.1 Relevance beats everything (the "why me?" test)
The first thing a stranger's brain asks is *"why is this person messaging ME?"*
If the answer is "they're blasting everyone," you're archived. The opener must
contain one detail that could only be about *them* — something from their bio,
their niche, their city, their content. MagnetEngine feeds the lead's bio into
the AI for exactly this reason. When you edit a DM, protect the personalized
line; it's the highest-value sentence in the message.

**Weak:** "Hey! Love your page 🔥"
**Strong:** "Hey Sara — saw you run online fitness coaching for new moms, that
postpartum angle is smart."

### 2.2 The curiosity gap
People reply to close open loops. Hint at a specific, relevant result without
explaining the whole mechanism — the explanation is what the call is for.

**Weak:** "We help coaches get more clients with our proven system."
**Strong:** "We helped another coach in your niche add 9 booked calls last
month without posting more content — different approach than what you're
probably doing."

Specific numbers (9 calls, 23 replies, 14 days) are more believable than round
ones and open a stronger loop.

### 2.3 Reciprocity — give before you ask
A micro-gift in the first exchanges (a genuine observation, a quick tip, a
relevant resource) creates a small social debt. It doesn't need to be big; it
needs to be *real*. Fake compliments register as spam instantly.

### 2.4 Social proof — "people like you"
Proof works when it's *proximate*: same niche, same size, same city. "We work
with 500 businesses" is noise. "We work with two other real-estate agents in
Miami" is signal. Keep one proximate proof line ready per niche you target.

### 2.5 Micro-commitments (the yes-ladder)
Big asks get ignored; tiny asks get answered. End DM #1 with a question that
costs the lead almost nothing to answer — a yes/no or a one-word answer. Each
small "yes" makes the next, bigger ask (the call) feel consistent to them.

**Weak CTA:** "Want to hop on a call this week?" *(too big, too soon)*
**Strong CTA:** "Mind if I send over a quick idea?" / "Are you taking on new
clients right now?" / "Worth a shot in your business?"

### 2.6 Loss aversion — use with a light touch
People move to avoid missing out more than to gain. Frame around what's
slipping past them ("most coaches lose the lead in the first 24h of silence"),
never fake scarcity ("only 2 spots left!!"). One loss-framed line per
*sequence*, not per message.

## 3. Anatomy of a first DM that gets replies

Three sentences. Under ~40 words. No links. No price. One question mark.

| Line | Job | Psychology |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Personal hook | Answer "why me?" | Relevance |
| 2. Value hint | Open the loop | Curiosity + proximate proof |
| 3. Micro-ask | Make replying effortless | Yes-ladder |

**Example (fitness coach lead):**
> Hey Sara — saw you coach new moms through postpartum fitness, love that
> niche. We helped another mom-focused coach add 9 booked calls last month
> without extra content. Open to hearing how?

**Checklist before you hit Approve in the queue:**
- [ ] Contains one detail specific to this lead (not swappable to anyone else)
- [ ] No link, no price, no "book here" in message #1
- [ ] Under ~40 words, reads like a human typed it on their phone
- [ ] Ends with a low-effort question
- [ ] Tone matches your brand voice (set in Settings → AI Prompt Wizard)

## 4. When they reply — speed, triage, and the call ask

### Speed to lead
Reply within the hour whenever possible. Response interest decays fast —
a warm reply left overnight is a cold lead again. Check your queue and
notifications daily at a set time (calendar block it).

### Triage every reply into one of three lanes
Log the sentiment in your CRM tracker immediately:

1. **Positive** ("tell me more", "how does it work?") → answer briefly, then
   ask for the call within 1–2 messages. Do not keep selling in the DM — every
   extra message is a chance to lose them.
2. **Neutral** ("who are you?", "is this a bot lol") → be disarming and human,
   re-anchor on the personal detail, restate the value hint in one line, ask a
   smaller question.
3. **Negative** ("not interested") → one graceful exit line, mark it in the
   CRM, move on. "All good — if it ever becomes a priority, you know where I
   am 🤝". A classy exit occasionally *reopens* the door weeks later.

### The call ask (the second conversion)
When interest is confirmed, make booking feel small and concrete:

> "Easiest way is a quick 15–20 min call — I'll walk you through exactly what
> we'd do for your account. Here's my calendar: [link]. What does your week
> look like?"

Psychology at work: **concreteness** (15–20 min, "walk you through"),
**autonomy** ("what does your week look like?" keeps them in control), and the
calendar link only appears *after* they've shown interest — links earn their
place, they're never given away first.

**After they book:** confirm same-day, and send a one-line reminder the
morning of the call. No-shows drop dramatically with a personal reminder
("looking forward to 3pm today 👊").

## 5. Follow-up psychology (where most of the money is)

Most booked calls come from follow-ups, not first messages. People aren't
ignoring you — they're busy. Set these in **Follow-Ups** inside MagnetEngine:

| Touch | Timing | Angle | Psychology |
|---|---|---|---|
| Follow-up 1 | +2–3 days | "Floating this back up" + restate value hint in new words | Mere exposure — familiarity builds trust |
| Follow-up 2 | +4–5 days after FU1 | Add something NEW: a result, an insight about their niche, a specific observation about their content | Reciprocity — each touch gives, never just "bumps" |
| Follow-up 3 | +5–7 days after FU2 | The breakup: close the loop politely and take the offer away | Loss aversion — takeaways trigger action |

**Breakup example:**
> "Last one from me, promise 🙂 — going to assume growth is handled for now.
> If that changes, my door's open. Rooting for you either way."

The breakup message routinely out-replies every other touch. Never send a
naked "bump" / "just following up!" — a follow-up with no new value is a
withdrawal, not a deposit.

## 6. The don'ts (instant-archive triggers)

- ❌ Link or calendar drop in message #1
- ❌ Price talk in the DMs — price lives on the call
- ❌ Walls of text (if it needs scrolling, it needs editing)
- ❌ Fake personalization ("love your content!" with zero specifics)
- ❌ Pitching the full offer before they've asked a single question
- ❌ Arguing with a "no" — exit gracefully, protect the brand
- ❌ More than 3 follow-ups — past that you're training them to ignore you
- ❌ Sending outside human hours — keep the drip inside 9am–9pm *their* time

## 7. Know your numbers (and what to fix)

Your dashboard tracks the funnel; your CRM tracker holds the detail. Benchmarks
for cold IG outreach:

| Metric | Healthy | If you're below it, fix… |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | 10–20% | The opener: more relevance, tighter hook, better lead filtering (Settings → Lead Filtering Rules) |
| Positive reply rate | 30–50% of replies | Targeting: you're reaching the wrong niche/size accounts |
| Booking rate | 20–40% of positive replies | The call ask: asking too late, too big, or not concrete enough |
| Show-up rate | 60–80% of booked | Reminders: confirm same-day + morning-of message |

Work **one** number at a time, top of the funnel first. Change one variable
per week (opener, audience, or follow-up timing), read the results in the
dashboard's 7-day chart, keep the winner.

## 8. Daily operating rhythm (30–45 min/day)

1. **Scrape** (2×/week): Campaign → new search in your niche → add to queue
2. **Approve** (daily, 15 min): review AI DMs, apply the §3 checklist, approve
3. **Send**: the extension drips approved DMs at your configured delay/cap
4. **Reply** (daily, 15 min): triage replies (§4), push warm leads to the call
5. **Log** (2 min): update the CRM tracker — replied, booked, cash collected
6. **Review** (weekly): check the funnel numbers (§7), adjust one variable

Consistency beats intensity: 20 great DMs a day, every day, out-earns 200
sloppy ones on a random Tuesday.
