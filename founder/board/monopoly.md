# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **A small market is named.** SMMA owners at $10k–50k/month is the right kind of small, specific market to try to own first. It is still unvalidated.
- **The positioning is clear.** "Written from this prospect's profile, approved by a human" separates it from the $47–97/month template bots. On quality per DM it may be much better than that category. It is not much better than a good VA.
- **The whole loop is in one product.** Find, write, approve, send, inbox, follow-up ladder and funnel are built and work end to end. Most rivals in reach probably cover only part of this, but that is an assumption to check, not a known fact.
- **The economics fit a solo owner.** Software with no services attached, at $147/month, could get to a large share of a niche without hiring.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage, and the ground is rented.** Every key part belongs to someone else: the AI models, the scraping API (HikerAPI), and Instagram itself, whose Terms of Service the sending breaches. The approval queue, the per-profile prompt and the human-pace sending are good craft, but a competent copycat could rebuild them in a few months. Nothing here is about ten times better on a hard-to-copy axis, and there is no network effect, scale advantage or brand yet. In ten years the position rests on Instagram's goodwill, which the product works against.
2. **It competes in a crowded category, not a monopoly.** It sells into the space shared by Instagram DM bots, AI SDR tools, VAs and appointment setters. The owner frames the buyer's choice as a trade-off between quality and volume, so buyers will compare on price, and the 7-day guarantee is already gone. Who exactly sells this within reach of the same buyer is an open question; no competitor names are in the brief.
3. **There is no distribution plan.** The brief has no channel, no marketing budget, no CAC, no runway and no paying-customer count. The extension is not in the Chrome Web Store. A product with no repeatable way to reach the first 100 customers is not a business yet.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many of the 7 founding seats are paying, and what has each one booked?
- How exactly do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost?
- What is the secret? What do you know about cold DM selling that a well-funded copycat does not?
- Name the five closest tools an SMMA owner would look at instead. Where do you beat each one by a wide margin, and where do you not?
- What builds up over time and is hard to copy: data on which DMs win in each niche, switching costs, a community, a brand?
- If Instagram blocks the extension's sending pattern or the scraping source next quarter, what is left?
- What is the gross margin per customer once the scraping and AI costs are measured?
- Why now? What changed in the last year that makes this work today and not two years ago?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
The vote is PASS, so there are no funding conditions. These are the changes that would move it to FUND IF:
- Proof of a small monopoly: a clear, measured lead among SMMA owners, such as most paying customers coming from that segment, with booked calls to show for it.
- A named, repeatable channel with a measured CAC well below the customer's lifetime value.
- A start on an advantage that grows with use, for example results data pooled across customers that makes each new DM measurably better and that a copycat cannot rebuild.
- A written plan for surviving if Instagram or the scraping provider cuts access.
