# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF
## What is strong
- **The market passes three of four tests.** Buyers selling $1,000+/mo retainers or $2,000+ projects feel the pain of prospecting, have money to spend, and can be reached on the same channel the product works in. Whether the market is growing is an open question.
- **The dream outcome is concrete and worth a lot.** A booked discovery call is the unit of success. At those deal sizes, one closed client a month pays for the plan several times over.
- **The pitch is about less effort.** About 10–15 minutes of review a day replaces a day of prospecting at 4–5 minutes a lead. It also takes away work buyers hate: finding leads, writing DMs, following up.
- **It is not competing on price.** It costs more than the $47–97 template bots and much less than a $2,400/mo setter or a $3,000/mo agency. The difference is a quality claim (one DM per profile, approved by a human), not a lower price.
## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is close to zero.** There are no testimonials, case studies or measured results. There is no guarantee, because the 7-day refund was retired. The trial lasts 3 days, needs a card and bills automatically, and payments are final. Every one of those pushes risk onto the buyer at the exact moment they have the least reason to believe it works. Of the four parts of the value equation, likelihood is where this offer fails.
2. **The biggest sacrifice is never addressed.** The buyer puts their own Instagram account, their main sales asset, at risk of restriction under the platform's terms. On top of that, the tab has to stay open for about 3.7 hours a day at the default cap, and the extension is a sideloaded file. The offer does nothing to lower or share that risk, so the effort and sacrifice side of the equation is heavy.
3. **The offer's numbers do not add up.** The plan gives 1,500 DMs but only 500 leads a month. The price has moved between $97, $197 and $147. The $497 anchor is not a price anyone has paid. Gross margin is unknown because per-customer API cost has never been measured. You cannot build a stack or a guarantee you can afford until you know what each customer costs to serve.
## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many of the 7 "claimed" founding seats are paying, and what did each one get (DMs sent, replies, calls booked)?
- What does one active customer cost you per month in scraping and AI?
- How long from signup to the first booked call? Can that happen inside a 3-day trial at 40 sends a day?
- Why was the money-back guarantee retired: refund abuse, chargebacks, or caution?
- What share of trial users finish setup (extension installed, tab left open)?
- What makes the 100-seat cap real, and what changes after seat 100?
- If you doubled the price, what would have to be in the box for a $10k/month SMMA owner to still say yes at once?
## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Get proof before scaling spend.** Bring on a small group of beachhead users (SMMA owners at $10k–50k/month) in exchange for permission to publish their real numbers. Funding depends on real, publishable results.
2. **Add a guarantee tied to the buyer's effort.** For example: approve and send a set number of DMs in 30 days, and if you get no booked call, receive a refund or a free month. Price it only after per-customer cost is measured.
3. **Build the stack around the sacrifice.** Offer done-with-you onboarding, a Chrome Web Store install, and published guidance on account warm-up and pacing. Make no safety promise.
4. **Fix the offer math.** Make the lead and DM quotas match, settle on one price, and either drop the $497 anchor or make it a real higher tier.
5. **Keep the scarcity real.** The seat count should come from actual signups, or the scarcity framing goes.
