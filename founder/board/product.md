# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The one thing is already named, and it is a good one.** "Every DM is written from that prospect's own profile, and a human approves it before it sends." That is one sentence, it is specific, and it is the reverse of the $47–97 template-bot category. Most products at this stage cannot say what they are in a line. This one can.
- **The experience it describes is the right one.** The moment that matters is 10–15 minutes of reviewing drafts in place of a day of prospecting. The approval queue is the right centre for the product. It is where the buyer's judgement and the product's work meet.
- **It removes a seam before the buyer ever sees it.** The Operator never handles an API key, and the Owner pays for scraping and AI. For a buyer with low-to-moderate technical comfort, that cut is worth a lot.
- **Restraint shows in places nobody checks.** Sends are paced like a human, nothing promises the account is safe, and scarcity is only allowed if it is real. That is care spent where the customer cannot look.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The last step happens on someone else's platform, against its rules.** Sending runs through Instagram, and automated DMs breach its Terms of Service. If the account is restricted, the buyer loses the asset they sell through. Nothing in the product can fix that, and it happens at the moment the product is supposed to pay off.
2. **The first five minutes are hard work.** The extension is a downloaded file, not a Web Store install. Sending only runs while an instagram.com tab stays open, about 3.7 hours a day at the 40/day default. The dashboard and the extension can update on different days. Every one of these is a handoff between systems, and that is where the experience breaks for a non-technical buyer, before they ever see a reply.
3. **It does too many things to do the one thing brilliantly.** The product has nine scrape sources, an AI inbox, an autopilot, follow-up ladders and a funnel dashboard. An autopilot that sends replies without a human reading them works against the "a human approves it" promise the product is positioned on. The offer doesn't add up either: 1,500 DMs a month against 500 leads a month, and a 40/day default gives about 1,200 sends in a 30-day month.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- From "pay" to "first approved DM sent", how many minutes and how many steps does it take a new buyer today? How many trial users get through it in 3 days? (Unknown.)
- Of trial users, how many sent at least one DM? (Unknown.)
- How many Operator accounts have been restricted, and what does the product do when that happens? (Unknown.)
- What share of AI drafts are approved without an edit? That number tells you whether the one thing works. (Unknown.)
- Which features would you cut if you could keep only the approval queue plus one way to find leads?
- Why does autopilot exist inside a product sold on human approval?
- Are any of the 7 founding seats paying? (Unknown.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Get the extension into the Chrome Web Store and measure time to first send.** Publish a target: first approved DM sent inside the 3-day trial for most buyers, with the real rate reported.
2. **Cut down to the one thing.** Keep a small number of lead sources (the ones buyers actually use), the approval queue and the inbox. Hide or remove autopilot unless it can be squared with the human-approval promise.
3. **Fix the offer so the numbers agree.** Leads, DMs and daily caps should describe one realistic month.
4. **Show the draft quality is real.** Measure the approval-without-edit rate and the reply rate on real accounts. Keep publishing nothing about results until then.
5. **Design the restriction moment.** Decide what the buyer sees, and what the product does, when Instagram pushes back.
